01 Channel Priority

You run a mouthguard program through orthodontists and you're exploring amateur leagues, tournaments, and clubs as affiliate partners. The email campaign needs to know which list to build first and how each model defines the buyer.

01Which channel should the email campaign hit first, orthodontic practices or amateur sports affiliates, and what split of effort? (e.g. 70/30 ortho)

Decides which list we build and send to first. A rough percentage is fine.

02In one or two sentences each: who is the buyer in the ortho model, and who is the buyer in the affiliate model? Name the role that signs off.

This is the person we put in the send list, so be precise about the role.

02 The Account Profile

What a qualified company looks like. Every answer here becomes a filter. Give numbers and named examples.

03Name 5 to 10 real orthodontic practices or groups that are perfect fits, current partners or dream targets. List them.

Named accounts are the seed we reverse-engineer the whole list from. Even just practice names and cities.

04What size practice qualifies? Give a number: chairs, doctors, locations, or staff count. Where's the floor and ceiling?

We filter on employee/location count directly, so a range beats "small to mid-size."

05Single-location practices, multi-location groups, or DSOs, which converts best, and do you want all three in the list or just one?

Independent practice and DSO are two different list builds with different titles.

06Does the practice need a specific scanner (iTero, Medit, others) or to be a digital/Invisalign provider for your program to work?

If yes, this is the single strongest qualifier and we hunt for it specifically.

07For the affiliate side, what org types and sports qualify? Rank them, and give a size floor (min players, families, or event participants).

Hockey, lacrosse, rugby, wrestling, BJJ, football. Which buy, and how big to be worth a send?

08Name 5 to 10 real leagues, clubs, tournaments, or circuits that would be ideal affiliate partners.

Named orgs let us find the lookalikes. Wins, losses, and dream targets all count.

09Geography: which states or metros should we prioritize, and are any off-limits? Same answer or different for each channel?

A location filter sharpens the list and the send volume per region.

03 The Contact Profile

The exact person who receives the email. This determines who lands in the campaign, by literal title.

10List the exact job titles to email at an orthodontic practice. The literal words on LinkedIn or their email signature.

We match these title strings directly. Owner, Orthodontist, Practice Manager, etc.

11List the exact titles to email at an amateur sports org. These orgs have messy titles, so give us every variant you've seen.

President, Director, Tournament Director, Sponsorship Lead, volunteer roles, anything.

12Is there a gatekeeper we'll hit before the decision-maker? Who, and is it better to email them or go around?

Office managers and front-desk staff can route or block. Tells us who to actually send to.

13For DSOs or multi-location groups, does the decision sit at the practice or move up to a regional/corporate title? Name it.

Determines whether we email the office or the corporate entity.

04 Qualifying Signals

The public tells that an account is ready right now. We score the list on these so the best-fit accounts get emailed first.

14Think about your last few wins. What was true about that practice or org right before they said yes?

The event before the yes is the trigger we score the whole list against.

15What would you Google or check to know an ortho practice is a hot prospect today? Be literal about what you'd look for.

New scanner, new location, new associate, sports-family marketing, elective-services push.

16For amateur orgs, what timing windows or signals mean buy now? Registration season, tournament dates, a sponsor hunt?

Timing drives send timing. We want the email to land in the registration window.

17If you had to rank your top 3 qualifying signals across both channels, most predictive first, what are they?

Sets the scoring weights so the highest-intent accounts get sent to first.

05 Disqualifiers & Exclusions

Who to keep out of the campaign. Exclusions protect deliverability and save credits, so be blunt.

18Which practices look like a fit on paper but never convert or never reorder? Describe the pattern.

Anti-ICP. These become hard exclusion rules in the list build.

19Which amateur orgs are not worth emailing? Too small, wrong sport, no audience, no budget for it to matter?

Keeps the affiliate list from filling up with dead rec leagues.

20Any accounts, brands, or partners we must exclude? Existing customers, current partners, anyone off-limits or conflicted.

We suppress these so no one gets a cold email who shouldn't.

06 Sourcing & Email Reachability

Where these accounts cluster, and whether we can actually email them at volume. This makes or breaks a heavy campaign.

21Where do these practices and orgs cluster? Directories, associations, platforms, lists, anywhere you'd look manually.

AAO, state dental boards, Invisalign provider finder, USA Hockey, US Lacrosse, IBJJF, registration platforms.

22For the contacts you want, do they typically have a findable business email, or just a generic info@ / front-desk inbox?

Critical for a heavy send. Solo practices and youth-club volunteers often have no individual email, which changes the whole approach.

23Is a personal email (Gmail/Yahoo) acceptable to send to for these contacts, or do you only want verified business emails?

Many small-org decision-makers only have a personal address. Affects deliverability strategy and list size.

24Roughly how many qualified accounts exist in each channel? Your gut on total addressable count is fine.

Tells us whether this is a tight high-personalization send or a broad volume campaign.

25What one or two data points, if we had them on every account, would make you trust the list most? (e.g. confirmed scanner, roster size)

Tells us which enrichment to prioritize in Clay before a single email goes out.

26Anything we didn't ask that we'd regret not knowing before building the list?

Open floor. The thing you know that we don't.
